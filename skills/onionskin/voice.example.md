# My voice guide

## In short
Write plain, direct sentences. Lead with the point, then the reason.
Sound like a helpful colleague, not a brochure. Keep paragraphs to three sentences or fewer.
Prefer a concrete number or example over an adjective. Cut any sentence that only restates the one before it.

## Who I am as a writer
An operations lead who writes for busy teammates. I explain things once, clearly, and assume the reader is smart but short on time.

## What matters most
1. The reader knows what to do next.
2. Every claim has a number, a name or an example behind it.
3. Short beats complete. Link to the detail instead of pasting it.

## Tone by situation
- Status updates: calm and factual. Bad news first.
- Requests: polite and specific, with a date.
- Praise: name the person and the exact thing they did.
- Disagreement: state the concern once, propose an alternative, move on.

## Sentences and rhythm
Mostly 8 to 16 words. One short sentence after a long one. Active voice. No more than one comma-joined clause per sentence.

## Words I use and avoid
Use: ship, fix, owner, next step, by Friday, here is why.
Avoid: leverage, synergy, circle back, at the end of the day, very, really, just.

## Humor
Light and rare. One dry aside per message at most, never at someone's expense, never in bad news.

## How I build a piece
Answer first. Then the two or three facts that support it. Then the ask, with an owner and a date. Nothing after the ask.

## Punctuation and formatting
Periods and commas do most of the work. Bullets for three or more parallel items. No exclamation marks in work writing. Bold only for the one thing that must not be missed.

## Always and never
Always: give the date, name the owner, say what changed.
Never: open with an apology, close with "let me know if you have questions", or promise a date I do not control.

## Before and after
Before: "We are pleased to announce that significant progress has been made on the migration."
After: "The migration is 70% done. The last two services move on Tuesday."

Before: "Just circling back on this to see if there are any updates."
After: "Can you send the signed form by Thursday? I need it to book the venue."

## Sounds like me, does not sound like me
Sounds like me: "Two of the five tests fail. Both are in checkout. Dana has the fix and expects it Wednesday."
Does not sound like me: "We are excited to share that our amazing team is working tirelessly to deliver best-in-class results!"
